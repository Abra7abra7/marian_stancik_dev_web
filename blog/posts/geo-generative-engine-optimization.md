### GEO: How to Get Cited by ChatGPT, Perplexity and Google AI Overviews

  

Direct answer: GEO is optimizing content so that generated answers (ChatGPT, Perplexity, Google AI Overviews) pick it up and cite it. Three pillars: the direct answer to the question right at the start, verifiability (structure, facts, sources, author) and technical readability (schema, llms.txt, clean HTML).

  

Classic SEO fights for position in a list of links. GEO solves a different situation: an AI reads ten pages and cites three. The number one spot in search means nothing if the model does not cite you. That changes both how you write and how you prepare the site technically.

  

### What GEO is and how it differs from SEO
  

SEO optimizes for the click: rank, meta description, preview. GEO optimizes for the model using your content: whether the answer to the question is right in the text, whether facts are precise and traceable, whether the author and the entity are clearly identifiable. The biggest difference: the model does not cite a page, it cites an answer. If your answer is buried in the fifth paragraph, a competitor with the answer in the first paragraph wins.

  

### How AI picks sources
  

The consensus across GEO guides (Backlinko, Enrich Labs) agrees on four properties of cited content:
  
    
- Specificity. Concrete numbers, dates, names and steps instead of generic phrases.
    
- Structure. Question → answer in H2/H3, short paragraphs, lists.
    
- Entity and author. Clear schema.org entities and a byline — the model needs to know who is speaking.
    
- Freshness. Updated pages with a visible date have a better chance of being cited.
  

  

### 8 practical steps
  
    
- The direct answer in the first paragraph. Answer the main question of the article in the first two sentences, then expand.
    
- Question → answer in H2/H3. Phrase the H2 as a question people actually ask; put the answer right under the heading.
    
- Schema.org Article/Person/Organization. JSON-LD with author, date and publisher entity; drop Product/Offer if you are not selling.
    
- llms.txt. A list of key pages and articles in the llmstxt.org standard at the site root; models read it as a map of the site.
    
- Consistent sameAs. The same profiles (X, LinkedIn, GitHub) linked in the schema and in llms.txt — an inconsistent entity weakens trust.
    
- Author and E-E-A-T. A byline with a name and a link to the author on every article; experience cannot be faked.
    
- Freshness and dates. A visible publish date and update date; refresh old articles instead of republishing them under a new date.
    
- Track citations. Once a month, check whether the models cite you for your key questions — GEO without measurement is whispering into the dark.

### Common myths
  

"GEO kills SEO." No. GEO extends SEO: without a solid technical base and readable content you have neither. "llms.txt is enough." No. llms.txt is a map; if the content behind it is not structured and verifiable, the map helps nobody. "Keywords no longer matter." They do — models map questions to content just like search engines, they just select the answer differently.
